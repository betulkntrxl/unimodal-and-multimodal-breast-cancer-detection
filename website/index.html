01 / The research

Question the complexity.

Combining imaging and clinical metadata adds complexity. These experiments investigate whether it adds predictive value, and which inputs models depend on.

01 — ESTABLISH

Build meaningful baselines

Evaluate individual data sources using traditional machine learning and neural networks. Compare structured-data classifiers with imaging and multimodal approaches.

02 — COMBINE

Bring modalities together

Investigate mammogram and metadata fusion, including transfer learning. Assess whether added inputs improve performance within the experimental setup.

03 — REMOVE & REPLACE

Test incomplete inputs

Remove imaging, remove clinical inputs, randomly drop a modality, or substitute GAN-generated images to explore dependence on available information.

04 / Interpreting the work

Context matters.

Strong metadata. Specific datasets.

The clinical branch includes BI-RADS and view information. High performance reflects the information already present in those features; it should not be interpreted as equivalent to detecting cancer from symptoms or raw images alone.

Research findings with boundaries.

The imaging dataset is small. Patient-level separation, independent external validation, and evaluation of metadata dependence are important considerations for interpreting generalisation. Scores across different datasets should not be treated as direct model rankings.
